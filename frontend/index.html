One discount cannot fix
four different problems

A customer leaving because the signal drops in Jaffna will take your free data and leave anyway. StaySignal works out which problem it actually is, then acts.

01

Detect

A trained model scores every customer 0–100 on the chance they go silent within 30 days, from reload gaps, data use and app activity.

02

Diagnose

It names the likely reason: a fault on their own tower, bill shock, a package too big or too small for them, a rival's promotion in their district, or plain loss of interest.

03

Act

The offer is chosen to match the reason and written as an SMS in Sinhala, Tamil or English — whichever that customer actually uses.

04

Track

The console counts who stayed and the rupees that stayed with them, so retention carries a number Hutch can check.

Why the model is
a simple one

The challenge requires the system to explain its decisions. A linear model makes that exact rather than approximate — a customer's risk is literally the sum of each feature's weighted contribution, which is what the panel above shows.

What it learned

Weights from logistic regression trained on 4,000 customers with 26 weeks of history each, labelled customers. Orange raises risk, grey lowers it.

Tuned for money, not accuracy

A retention offer gives away real margin, including to customers who were never going to leave. So the alert line is set where revenue is best protected — not where accuracy peaks.

Honest about where we are

The model is trained on simulated data, because the competition dataset had not been released when this was built. The pipeline is written so only the input file changes when it arrives. The offer cost and save rate are stated assumptions, not measured values — an A/B test would replace them.
